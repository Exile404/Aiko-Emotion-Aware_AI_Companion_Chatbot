### 3.6 Efficiency measurement methodology

**Instrumentation target.** All latency and memory figures were obtained by instrumenting the deployed runtime package directly, not a benchmark reimplementation. The harness imports the shipped `Memory`, `EmotionDetector`, `Transcriber`, `AikoChat` and `VoiceClient` classes and constructs them in the same order as the production orchestrator, so measured load times and resident memory reflect the configuration an end user actually runs. The reply path replicates the production method call for call, with one deliberate omission: the post-reply memory write is excluded, since it executes after the response has been returned and therefore cannot contribute to perceived latency. Memory retrieval is not stubbed; it queries the live vector store, so recall latency reflects the true collection size.

**Timing protocol.** GPU execution is asynchronous with respect to the host, so a naive host-side timer measures kernel launch overhead rather than computation. Every timed region is therefore closed with a device synchronisation before its end timestamp is taken, which additionally guarantees that the following region begins on an idle device. All GPU-resident stages are enclosed in such regions, so this chaining holds throughout the measured sequence. Each conversational turn is decomposed into speech recognition, emotion inference, memory recall, optional reasoning, response generation, and speech synthesis, timed independently. The first three turns of every configuration are discarded as warm-up and 20 turns are reported. Two costs are excluded from end-to-end latency and reported separately: the voice-activity detector's silence hang (0.5 s), which is a fixed policy parameter rather than computation, and the post-reply memory write described above.

**Input material.** To avoid measuring the system on text of its own choosing, inputs are drawn from the MELD test split, filtered to utterances of 1 to 6 s (23 clips, mean duration 2.53 s). Using held-out corpus audio rather than synthetic prompts keeps recognition and emotion inference on realistic input, and fixes the input distribution across configurations so that ablations differ only in the system under test.

**Memory attribution.** Total device memory is insufficient to substantiate a single-GPU claim, because a desktop session contributes an unrelated baseline. Occupancy is therefore attributed per process via the driver's compute-process query and partitioned into system-owned processes (the application process, the two language-model server processes, and the synthesis server) and unrelated processes (compositor, editor, browser). Reported footprint is the system-owned sum; the unrelated total is reported alongside it for transparency. This distinction is material: unrelated processes accounted for 483 MB, so an unpartitioned total overstates the system's requirement by approximately 3 % of the device.

**Cross-validation of measurements.** Response generation is measured along two independent paths: end to end through the orchestration framework, and directly against the inference server's HTTP interface, which additionally reports internal load, prompt-evaluation and generation intervals. The two agree to within 23 ms (329 ms against 306 ms), which bounds framework overhead and provides an internal consistency check on the timing protocol. The server's own accounting closes to within 6 ms of its reported total.

**Component decomposition caveat.** Emotion inference is reported both as a total and as voice and text components. Because the total invokes the same models the components measure, the total is timed first, on audio the models have not yet seen, and the component figures are collected afterward on warm state. The components are therefore diagnostic and are not summed to form the total; end-to-end latency uses the total exclusively.

**Reported statistics.** Mean, standard deviation, median and 95th percentile are reported per stage. The percentile is computed by nearest rank and, at twenty measured turns, rests on a single order statistic with one observation above it, so it is reported as an indication of tail behaviour rather than a stable estimate. Where only five repetitions were available, the maximum is reported instead, since a 95th percentile at that sample size is the maximum.

**Threats to validity.** Three measurements proved sensitive to conditions outside the system. Component load times vary with filesystem cache state (the emotion encoder's load ranged from 8.2 to 11.9 s across four runs with no change to the code), so load durations are reported as ranges and resident memory, which varied by under 1 % across the same runs, carries the cold-start claim instead. Synthesis latency measured in situ depends on the length of whatever the language model produced, and the decoder is itself stochastic, so identical input yielded 14.18 s and 16.19 s of audio in two runs; synthesis is therefore additionally characterised by a controlled length sweep (Section 3.7) and reported as a fitted rate rather than a point estimate. Finally, all figures are from a single hardware configuration (Section 3.1) and no cross-device generalisation is claimed.

### 3.7 Speech synthesis characterisation

**Rationale.** Synthesis is the largest single contributor to time-to-first-audio (Section 4.2), but measured in situ it is not a property of the synthesiser alone: chunk length is determined by whatever the language model produced on that turn, and generation is stochastic. In-situ synthesis latency consequently varied by more than 100 ms in its mean and 180 ms in its median across otherwise identical runs. Synthesis is therefore characterised separately, with the language model removed from the loop, so that the reported figures depend only on text length and are reproducible.

**Design.** A fixed pool of twelve in-domain reply sentences is concatenated until a target character count is reached, giving eight target lengths spanning 20 to 200 characters. Concatenation stops at the first sentence boundary at or beyond the target, so realised lengths overshoot their targets and span 30 to 221 characters; all analysis uses realised rather than target length. The sentence offset into the pool is advanced on each repetition so that no two repetitions synthesise identical text, which prevents any server-side caching from being mistaken for fast synthesis. Five repetitions are measured per length after two discarded warm-up repetitions. Synthesis is invoked through the same socket client the application uses, so the measurement includes the real inter-process transport rather than an in-process call.

**Quantities measured.** For each repetition the harness records synthesis wall time, output audio duration before pause trimming, output audio duration after pause trimming, and the cost of the trimming operation itself. Trimming is measured because it executes on the deployed critical path between synthesis and playback, and is therefore part of time-to-first-audio. The primary reported quantity is the realtime factor, defined as synthesis wall time divided by output audio duration, because it is invariant to text length and consequently transfers across utterances in a way absolute latency does not.

**Assessing synthesis faithfulness.** The chunk-length floor in the deployed configuration exists because the vocoder was observed to degrade on very short input, producing speech not present in the text. Testing whether that floor is justified requires an objective measure of such excess. Audio duration per character is the natural candidate, on the reasoning that faithful synthesis should produce duration proportional to text length. That reasoning is incorrect, and the correction matters for interpreting the result. Output duration is affine rather than proportional in text length, because each utterance carries a fixed non-speech component (onset and offset silence, and a terminal pause) that survives trimming. Per-character duration therefore declines hyperbolically with length even under perfectly faithful synthesis, and an elevated per-character value at short lengths is not by itself evidence of excess speech. Faithfulness is accordingly assessed in two steps. First, duration is measured both before and after pause trimming, which separates non-speech padding, removed by trimming, from speech content, which is not. Second, post-trimming duration is regressed on realised character count across the full sweep, and excess speech is tested as a positive residual at short lengths against that fitted relation rather than against a constant per-character expectation.

**Limitations.** Two constraints bound what this procedure can establish. Because concatenation overshoots its target, the shortest realised text was 30 characters, so the sweep does not reach the sub-20-character regime in which degradation was originally observed; the procedure can therefore fail to detect a defect confined to that regime, and no claim is made about it. Second, the synthesiser's decoder is stochastic, so repeated synthesis of identical text yields differing output durations (14.18 s against 16.19 s for the same 221-character input in two runs). Individual per-length means are correspondingly imprecise at five repetitions, and the reported characterisation is the fitted rate and realtime factor rather than any single per-length figure.

### 3.8 Emotion recognition evaluation protocol

**Corpus and label scheme.** Fusion is evaluated on MELD, which provides multi-party conversational video with utterance-level emotion annotation over seven categories that correspond one-to-one with the deployed label scheme, so no relabelling or class merging is required. Audio is extracted from the distributed video at 16 kHz mono. The corpus's own partition is used throughout: 9,988 training utterances for which extraction succeeded, 1,108 development utterances, and 2,610 test utterances. MELD is selected deliberately for its difficulty: it contains overlapping speakers, laughter track, and background music, and therefore represents in-the-wild conditions closer to a deployed companion than acted studio corpora.

**Unimodal inference.** The two encoders are the deployed ones, queried exactly as the runtime queries them, so that measured fusion behaviour applies to the shipped system. The voice encoder emits a nine-way distribution; its two non-emotional categories are folded into the neutral class rather than discarded, preserving total probability mass, yielding a seven-dimensional score vector. The text encoder emits seven-dimensional scores directly over the same scheme. Transcripts are the corpus's own, not recognised speech, so that fusion is evaluated independently of recognition error.

**Fusion feature representations.** Two representations are compared. The score-level representation concatenates the two seven-dimensional distributions into a fourteen-dimensional vector, which is the information a decision-level fusion rule can access and is what the deployed heuristic operates on. Because a seven-dimensional summary may discard voice information that its own classification head exploits, a second representation replaces the voice scores with the encoder's 1024-dimensional utterance embedding, giving 1024-dimensional voice-only and 1031-dimensional joint variants. This distinguishes two different negative outcomes: fusion failing because voice carries no complementary signal, and fusion failing because a score-level bottleneck destroys signal that is present.

**Fusion models.** Two learners are used, chosen to bracket model capacity rather than to be tuned. The first is multinomial logistic regression preceded by feature standardisation, which is linear and therefore cannot manufacture interaction structure that is not present. The second is histogram gradient boosting, which can represent non-additive interaction between the modalities and is the stronger learner given training-set scale. Both are cheap enough at inference that neither materially affects the efficiency results in Section 4, which is a requirement rather than an incidental property: a fusion rule that improved accuracy at the cost of the latency budget would not be deployable in this system.

**Partitioning and leakage control.** Fusion heads are fitted on the training split, all configuration and representation choices are made on the development split, and the test split is read once per reported configuration. Because a substantial part of the earlier exploratory work used cross-validation on the test split for feasibility, and because cross-validated estimates on conversational corpora are vulnerable to within-dialogue leakage, the two are compared directly rather than assumed equivalent: identical feature sets are evaluated under random stratified folds and under folds grouped by dialogue identifier, so that no dialogue appears in both fit and evaluation partitions. The difference between the two estimates quantifies leakage rather than leaving it as an untested caveat, and the direction of that difference is diagnostic, since lexical and topical overlap within a dialogue and speaker identity within a dialogue inflate the two modalities asymmetrically.

**Baselines.** Four comparators are reported: the deployed hand-tuned heuristic fusion rule; voice-only and text-only decisions taken as the encoder's own argmax; and learned single-modality heads, which isolate the contribution of the learned decision layer from the contribution of the second modality. The last is essential. Without it, a learned fusion head that beats an argmax baseline can be misread as evidence that fusion helps, when the gain may come entirely from learning a decision layer over one modality.

**Statistical testing.** Differences between configurations are assessed by paired bootstrap over test utterances, 1,000 resamples, reporting 95 % confidence intervals and two-sided p values. Pairing is on identical test items, so the comparison controls for item difficulty rather than comparing independent point estimates whose intervals would be wider and less informative.

**Covariate-shift probe.** One specific alternative explanation for a train-to-test performance drop is distributional difference between the splits. This is tested directly by training a discriminator to identify an utterance's split from its features alone and reporting its area under the ROC curve, where 0.5 indicates no detectable shift. The probe's scope is stated explicitly because it is easy to overread: it examines the marginal feature distribution only, and cannot exclude a change in the conditional relationship between features and labels.

**Metrics.** Weighted F1, macro F1 and accuracy are reported together throughout. MELD's class distribution is severely skewed, with the two rarest categories represented by only tens of development examples, so accuracy alone rewards majority-class behaviour and weighted F1 alone can conceal complete failure on rare classes. Macro F1 is reported specifically to expose that failure mode.

**Model selection as an object of study.** The development split is additionally used to record what a conventional selection procedure would have chosen, independently of what the test split subsequently shows. This is reported as a finding rather than discarded as a procedural detail, because the two diverge, and the divergence has methodological implications for published results on this corpus that select fusion architectures on this development split.

**Limitations.** Three bounds apply. Conclusions are corpus-specific: an absence of measurable benefit from the voice modality under these conditions does not generalise to clean acted speech, and a boundary condition is established separately in Section 4.5. Gold transcripts are used, so the text pathway is evaluated under conditions more favourable than deployment, which if anything makes the finding conservative with respect to the voice modality. Finally, the analysis is at the decision level; it does not exclude the possibility that representation-level or attention-based fusion trained end to end could extract complementary signal that neither representation tested here exposes.
